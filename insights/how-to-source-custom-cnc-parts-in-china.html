How to Source Custom CNC Parts in China

A practical B2B guide for international buyers on preparing technical packages, evaluating CNC suppliers, managing samples and controlling quality.

Sourcing custom CNC machined parts from China can deliver significant cost and capacity advantages when the process is structured correctly. Poor preparation or weak supplier selection often leads to delayed samples, dimensional issues and unexpected costs. This guide outlines a practical workflow used by experienced international buyers.

1. Prepare a Complete Technical Package Before Contacting Suppliers

What you send determines the quality of quotes and the suitability of the suppliers who respond. A complete RFQ package typically includes:

  • 2D drawings (PDF or DWG) with all critical dimensions, tolerances and surface-finish callouts
  • 3D models in STEP or IGES format for CAM programming and geometry verification
  • Exact material grade and condition (e.g., 6061-T6 aluminum, 304 stainless, 4140 steel)
  • Required surface finishes (anodizing type, plating, powder coat, as-machined Ra values)
  • Target quantities (prototype, pilot, annual volume) and delivery timeline
  • Any special inspection requirements (FAI, CMM reports, material certificates)

Incomplete packages force suppliers to make assumptions. Assumptions create risk.

2. Shortlist Suitable CNC Suppliers

Not every CNC shop is appropriate for every part. Key evaluation criteria include:

Machine capabilityAxis count, travel, spindle power must match part geometryRequest machine list with models and ages

3. Verify the Supplier Before Committing

Basic verification steps that reduce risk:

  1. Request a clear copy of the Chinese business license and the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code.
  2. Cross-check the company on the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (gsxt.gov.cn).
  3. Confirm the registered address matches the claimed factory location.
  4. Request a live video walkthrough of the production floor focusing on relevant machines.
  5. Ask for sample dimensional reports from previous similar parts.
  6. Consider a third-party factory audit (SGS, TÜV or equivalent) for higher-value or regulated projects.

Do not rely solely on platform badges or self-declared “Gold Supplier” status.

4. Manage Samples and First-Article Inspection

For most CNC projects, a prototype or pilot batch is essential before volume production. Typical sample lead times for parts that do not require new tooling range from 3–15 working days depending on complexity and queue.

Request a First Article Inspection (FAI) package that includes:

  • Full dimensional report against the drawing
  • Material test report / certificate of conformity
  • Surface finish measurements where specified
  • Photos of critical features and packaging

Approve the “golden sample” in writing and retain it as the quality benchmark for subsequent production.

5. Define Quality Control and Production Follow-up

Agree inspection points and acceptance criteria before mass production starts. Common practices include:

  • In-process checks at defined stages
  • Final inspection with sampling plan (AQL) or 100% for critical dimensions
  • Pre-shipment inspection (PSI) coordinated by the buyer or a third party
  • Clear non-conformance handling and rework rules

6. Working With a China-Based Sourcing Partner

Many international buyers prefer to work through a local partner who can:

  • Review drawings and recommend suitable processes and suppliers
  • Communicate technical details in Chinese and English
  • Coordinate sampling, production follow-up and quality checks on the ground
  • Handle packaging and shipment preparation

This approach reduces the need for the buyer to manage multiple factories directly while maintaining control over requirements and quality standards.

FAQ

How do I verify a CNC factory in China?
Request the business license and Unified Social Credit Code, check gsxt.gov.cn, review ISO certificates, request machine lists and sample inspection reports, and conduct a video factory tour or third-party audit when appropriate.
What is the typical MOQ for custom CNC parts?
Many shops accept prototype quantities from 1–50 pieces. Production MOQs commonly start in the 50–500 piece range depending on part complexity and material.
What files should I send for a CNC quote?
2D drawings with tolerances, 3D STEP/IGES models, material specification, surface finish requirements, quantities and any special inspection or packaging needs.
