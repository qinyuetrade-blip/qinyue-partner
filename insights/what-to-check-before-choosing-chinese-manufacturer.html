What to Check Before Choosing a Chinese Manufacturer

Capability, equipment, certifications, communication, quality systems and commercial terms — a practical checklist for international buyers.

Choosing the wrong manufacturer in China can lead to delayed samples, quality issues and wasted time. Before you commit, review the following areas systematically.

1. Product Capability

Does the supplier actually make products similar to yours? Ask for:

  • Examples of comparable parts or products (photos, drawings if possible)
  • Materials they regularly work with
  • Typical tolerances and complexity they handle

A factory strong in one category is not automatically suitable for another.

2. Equipment and Process

Request a machine or process list relevant to your product. For CNC, for example: axis count, travel, brands and approximate age. For injection molding or other processes, understand capacity and secondary operations (surface treatment, assembly).

3. Certifications and Quality Systems

ISO 9001 is a common baseline. Industry-specific systems (e.g. automotive, medical) matter when your product requires them. Ask for certificate numbers and, when risk justifies it, verify with the issuing body. Also ask how they document inspection — sample reports are more useful than claims alone.

4. Communication

Technical English and responsiveness reduce iteration time. Evaluate how clearly they answer questions about drawings, materials and process. Slow or vague replies early on often continue during production.

5. Commercial Terms

  • MOQ and pricing structure
  • Payment terms (avoid 100% upfront to personal accounts)
  • Lead times for samples and production
  • Who owns tooling if custom molds are needed

6. Samples Before Volume

Approve samples in writing and keep a golden sample. Do not skip this step for the sake of speed on a first order.

Is ISO 9001 enough?
It is a useful baseline but not a guarantee of fit for your specific product. Always match capability and process to your requirements and review actual sample quality.
Should I visit the factory?
On-site visits or third-party audits add confidence for higher-value projects. For many projects, structured video tours, sample evaluation and clear documentation are a practical starting point.
