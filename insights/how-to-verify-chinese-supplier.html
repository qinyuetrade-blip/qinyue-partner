How to Verify a Chinese Supplier Before Placing an Order

A practical 6-step checklist used by experienced international buyers to reduce supplier risk.

Skipping verification is one of the most common reasons buyers encounter quality problems, delivery failures or outright fraud when sourcing from China. The following steps can be applied to almost any supplier — manufacturer or trading company.

Step 1: Check the Business License

Every legitimate Chinese company has a business license (营业执照) with an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code. Ask for a clear copy and note:

  • Registered Chinese company name
  • Unified Social Credit Code
  • Registered address
  • Legal representative
  • Business scope
  • Establishment date and registered capital

A supplier who refuses to provide the license is a major red flag.

Step 2: Verify on the Official Government Database (GSXT)

Go to the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System: gsxt.gov.cn. Search by company name or credit code. Confirm:

  • The company exists and is in active status
  • Registered details match the license
  • There are no serious administrative penalties or abnormal operation flags

The site is in Chinese; use a translation tool or a Chinese-speaking colleague if needed.

Step 3: Verify Export Capability and Track Record

Ask for evidence of previous exports (bills of lading, customs records, or references from international clients). For manufacturers claiming to export directly, they should have a Customs Registration Code. Cross-check where possible using commercial databases or by requesting anonymised shipment references.

Step 4: Confirm Factory vs Trading Company

Neither is automatically better — but you need to know which one you are dealing with. Ask for:

  • Factory address (not just office address)
  • Photos or video of production equipment relevant to your product
  • Machine list or production capacity description

A live video call walking the production floor is one of the highest-value verification steps available remotely.

Step 5: Review Certifications and Quality Systems

Request copies of relevant certificates (ISO 9001, industry-specific systems, product certifications). Verify certificate numbers with the issuing body when the risk justifies it. Ask for sample inspection reports from previous production to see how the supplier actually documents quality.

Step 6: Start with Samples and a Trial Order

Never place a large first order without approved samples. Keep the approved “golden sample” as the quality benchmark. For the first production run, consider a smaller quantity or staged payment, and arrange a pre-shipment inspection when the order value or risk warrants it.

Additional Practical Tips

  • Payment should go to a company bank account that matches the contracting entity — avoid personal accounts.
  • Use clear written specifications, drawings and quality agreements.
  • For higher-risk projects, engage a third-party auditor (SGS, TÜV, Intertek or a trusted local partner).
  • Document everything: quotes, sample approvals, inspection reports and communications.

FAQ

How do I check if a Chinese company is real?
Request the business license and Unified Social Credit Code, then verify on gsxt.gov.cn. Confirm the registered name, address and status match the supplier’s claims.
Is a third-party factory audit necessary?
For higher-value, regulated or first-time large orders, yes. For smaller or lower-risk projects, a structured video tour plus sample evaluation may be sufficient.
